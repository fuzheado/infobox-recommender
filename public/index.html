About the infobox recommender

This tool helps answer a question every Wikipedia editor eventually faces: “this article has no infobox — should it have one, and if so which template?” Rather than guessing from the article itself, it runs a census over the article’s peers — the articles most like it — and reports what infobox practice actually looks like there.

How it works

  1. Discover peers from three signals: Wikidata P31 (instance-of) siblings (articles of the same class, across languages), the article’s most specific categories (smallest by member count — the sharpest genre pointers), and WikiProject banners on the talk page (which project standardizes this subject’s infobox).
  2. Census: for every peer, fetch its template list and count infobox-family templates — what share of peers have any infobox, and which templates dominate.
  3. Decide: turn the census into one of three verdicts, each shipped with its evidence.

The three verdicts

Recommend: {{Infobox election}} No infobox customary Weak signal
  • Recommend — a strong majority of same-class peers use this specific template (e.g. 30 of 31 imperial-election articles use {{Infobox election}}).
  • No infobox customary — peers are overwhelmingly bare; adding one would make the article the outlier (trace fossils: 1% of peers boxed).
  • Weak signal — evidence is mixed or too thin; the tool abstains rather than guesses and points you to the WikiProject banner for the standardized answer.

Reading the report

Every verdict carries its evidence: the share of peers with an infobox (coverage), the template distribution with bars, the same-class vs category-only split, and examples of boxed and bare peers — so you can weigh the argument yourself. Every template name links to its documentation, and every report has a shareable URL you can paste into a talk-page discussion.

Why is this advisory?

“The use of infoboxes is neither required nor prohibited for any article. Whether to include an infobox… is determined through discussion and consensus among the editors at each individual article.”
— Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Infoboxes (MOS:INFOBOXUSE)

MOS explicitly weighs arguments based on what similar articles do. This tool produces exactly that evidence — it never edits articles and never decides. About 77% of enwiki articles have an infobox; the remaining ~23% include many that are appropriately without one.

How well does it work?

The pipeline is evaluated against 64 labeled cases drawn from the infobox-request backlog (labels = templates editors actually chose): 35 correct / 6 near-miss or defensible disagreements / 23 honest abstentions — 85% accuracy on decisive verdicts.
